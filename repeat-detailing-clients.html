How to get repeat detailing clients

Most detailers spend their time and money chasing new clients while the ones they already have drift off to whoever they see next. Here's the simple system we use to bring them back: no ads, no discounts, one text.

Updated November 11, 2026 · 6-minute read

The math on the clients you already have

Say you've detailed 40 clients over the last couple of years, and the average job was $200. If just 1 in 4 of them books you again 3 times a year:

40 past clients × 1 in 410 clients
10 clients × 3 visits a year30 jobs
30 jobs × $200$6,000 a year

No ads, no flyers, no Facebook posts. These people already know your work, already have your number and already said yes once. The only thing standing between you and that money is a text you didn't send.

Your easiest money is the client you already have.

What are your past clients worth?

About $6,000 a year from people who already know you.

Change the numbers to yours. Even at half these numbers, it's real money.

Step 1: Keep a real client list

Not just a phone full of contacts. For every client you need six things: name, phone, vehicle, the date of their last detail, what you did, and what they paid. Without the date and the service, you can't tell who's due. Without the price, you can't tell who's worth calling first.

If your old jobs live in Square, a notebook or your texts, pull them together once. It takes an evening and it pays for years.

Step 2: Know when each client is due

Different jobs wear off at different speeds. Use these as a starting point:

Last serviceDue again in about
Wash or maintenance wash1 month
Interior or exterior detail3 months
Full detail4 months
Polish, paint correction or coating6 months

Some clients want every month, some once a year. Ask at the end of the job: "Want us to reach out in about four months?"

Step 3: Send one friendly text

No sales pitch, no coupon. Just a reminder that sounds like you:

"Hi Mike! It's Shine Pros. It's been about 4 months since we detailed your Honda Pilot. A great time to get it looking fresh again. Want us to get you on the schedule? Just reply with a day that works for you!"

It works because it's personal (their name, their vehicle), it's useful (it's been a while, and they know it) and it's easy to answer (just a day).

Step 4: Follow up once, then let it go

No answer? Wait about two weeks and send one short follow-up. Still nothing, leave them alone until next season. People are busy, not annoyed. Two texts a season is a reminder; five is a reason to block you.

Step 5: Make booking the easy part

When they reply "sure, what do you have?", give them two days to pick from, or a link where they can see the price and choose a day themselves. Every back-and-forth text is a chance for "let me check and get back to you" and you never hear from them again.

Lessons that cost detailers the most

Waiting for clients to call. They mean to. Then the car gets dirty, life happens, and they book whoever's ad they see first.

Discounting to win them back. They came to you at full price. A reminder brings them back; a discount just teaches them to wait for one.

Texting the whole list on the same day. Ten replies at once and you can't fit them all in. Send a few a day, the ones most due first.

Not knowing who's worth calling. A $450 full-detail client who's 5 months out is worth more of your time than a $60 wash from last week.
