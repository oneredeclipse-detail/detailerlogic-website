Pricing guide

How to charge a travel fee for mobile detailing

Most mobile detailers either charge every client the same mobile fee or don't charge for the drive at all. Both leave money in the truck. Here's how we price travel so the far jobs pay for the drive and the close ones stay happy.

Updated November 11, 2026 · 5-minute read

What a far-away job really costs you

A client books a $300 detail 25 miles from home. That's 50 miles round trip, about an hour and a quarter behind the wheel in normal traffic.

That hour and a quarter is time you could have spent on a job. If your hour is worth $75, that's close to $94 you worked for and gave away, before you've spent a cent on gas. Do that twice a week and it's over $9,000 a year.

Your drive time is work time. Price it like work.

Three ways to charge for travel

1. A flat mobile fee. Every mobile job pays the same extra, say 10% or $25. Simple, but the client 3 miles away pays the same as the one 30 miles away. You overcharge the close ones and lose money on the far ones.

2. Dollars per mile after a free zone. No charge inside your free miles, then a set amount for every mile past it. Easy to explain: "First 15 miles are free, $2 a mile after that."

3. A percentage of the job per mile. Same free zone, but each extra mile adds a small percentage of the job. It scales with the size of the job: a $600 coating 30 miles out pays more for the trip than a $90 wash, which makes sense, because the bigger job is worth the drive.

Step 1: Set your free zone

Draw the circle where you'd happily go for no extra charge, usually where most of your clients already are. For a lot of mobile detailers that's 10 to 20 miles from home. Inside it, no travel line at all. That's your "we come to you" promise.

Step 2: Pick your rate past the zone

Free zone 15 miles, client 25 miles away (one way):

$2 a mile × 10 extra miles$20
Or 1% a mile × 10 extra miles on a $300 job$30

Count miles one way from your base. Most clients understand "it's 25 miles from us"; nobody wants to argue about your drive home.

Step 3: Set a max distance

Past a certain distance, no fee makes the day work: the drive eats the slot for your next job. Pick a number and stick to it. "We travel up to 40 miles" protects your schedule and sounds professional, not picky.

Step 4: Say it before they book

Put the rule in writing where clients see it before they ask: your booking page, your price list, your first reply. "First 15 miles free, then $2 a mile, up to 40 miles." A fee they knew about is a detail; a fee they find on the invoice is a complaint.

Lessons that cost detailers the most

Burying travel in the price. Then your close clients overpay and shop around, and your far ones still don't cover the drive.

Counting only gas. Fuel is the small part. The hour in the truck is the big one.

No max distance. One 60-mile job can cost you a second job that day.

Working it out by hand for every client. Looking up the distance, doing the math, typing the quote: it's ten minutes per lead, and leads don't wait.
